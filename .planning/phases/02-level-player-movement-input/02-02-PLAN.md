<objective>
Give the world a player and a heartbeat: the vector camera pose, per-axis radius collision that slides along walls, and the requestAnimationFrame loop with a clamped delta-time. This is the plan where the two most silently-breakable properties in the project — frame-rate independence and no-tunneling collision — become mechanically asserted facts.

## Phase Goal

The player can move, strafe, run, and look around a hand-designed level with correct wall collision, validated on a top-down view before any 3D exists. (Phase 2's ROADMAP Goal is stated as an outcome rather than an "As a / I want to / so that" user story — see the planner return note.)

Purpose: The camera model chosen here is what Phase 3's raycaster is built on (D-01), and delta-time is the one thing PITFALLS calls out as unretrofittable — "bake dt in from first movement" — because retrofitting it later touches every motion call site (D-05). Per-axis radius collision is table stakes; combined-axis collision "feels terrible" and players notice immediately (D-06).
Output: `js/player.js` and `js/game.js` (the `Player` and `Game` globals), `CONFIG.DT_MAX`, a booting game loop, and a headless motion harness that proves the clamp and the no-tunneling property.
</objective>

  <files>Doom/Claude Opus 4.8/GSD/js/player.js, Doom/Claude Opus 4.8/GSD/js/config.js</files>
  <behavior>
    - Spawning from the level puts the player at Level.playerStart with a unit direction and a camera plane of length CONFIG.FOV_PLANE that is perpendicular to it.
    - Rotating by any angle keeps the direction a unit vector, keeps the plane at length FOV_PLANE, keeps the two perpendicular, and preserves the plane-from-direction relationship exactly — after a thousand rotations as after one.
    - Holding forward for one simulated second moves the player WALK_SPEED cells along the direction vector, whether that second is delivered in 60 frames or in 500.
    - Holding forward and strafe together moves at exactly the same speed as holding forward alone (the intent vector is normalized), just in a diagonal direction.
    - The run modifier multiplies speed by RUN_MULT and nothing else.
    - Running head-on into a wall stops forward progress at the collision radius and leaves the player in a non-solid cell.
    - Running diagonally into a wall keeps the free axis moving and zeroes only the blocked axis (wall sliding).
    - The Y attempt is evaluated against the x the player actually holds after the X attempt has been accepted or rejected — so sliding along a wall the X move just bounced off still resolves Y from the real, current position.
    - After any sequence of frames, floor(x)/floor(y) is never a solid cell.
    - Within a single frame the player never skips a cell on either axis, and a frame that changes both cell coordinates always leaves the intermediate cell it passed through non-solid.
  </behavior>
  <action>
Add `DT_MAX: 0.05` to `CONFIG` in `js/config.js` with a comment naming it as the delta-time clamp that bounds the per-frame step (D-05). Change nothing else in config.js.

Create `js/player.js` attaching a global `Player` object. No DOM, no timers, no rendering — pure simulation state and math, so it runs unchanged in a Node vm sandbox.

STATE. `x`, `y` (world position, in cells), `dirX`, `dirY` (unit direction), `planeX`, `planeY` (camera plane). Tuning constants on the object with a one-line comment each: `RADIUS` 0.22 (collision radius in cells), `WALK_SPEED` 3.0 (cells per second), `RUN_MULT` 1.8, `TURN_SPEED` 2.6 (radians per second for keyboard turning), `MOUSE_SENSITIVITY` 0.0022 (radians per raw mouse pixel — event-based, deliberately NOT scaled by delta-time, per the PITFALLS note that mouse input is already per-event).

CAMERA MODEL (D-01 — locked, Phase 3 depends on it). The plane is always the direction rotated a quarter turn and scaled by the field of view: `planeX = dirY * CONFIG.FOV_PLANE` and `planeY = -dirX * CONFIG.FOV_PLANE`. Expose `Player.setDir(dx, dy)` which normalizes the given vector, stores it, and recomputes the plane from it — the single place that relationship is written. Expose `Player.rotate(a)`: compute `cos(a)` and `sin(a)` ONCE and apply the SAME 2D rotation matrix to both the direction and the plane (rotating both, rather than recomputing the plane, is what keeps this the standard raycaster camera and avoids per-column trig later). Positive `a` turns right. Do not store an authoritative angle; if a convenience angle is wanted for the top-down view, derive it on demand from the direction vector.

SPAWN. `Player.spawn()` reads `Level.playerStart` and sets `x`, `y` from it, then calls `setDir` with the start facing. If `Level.playerStart` is missing, fall back to the centre of the first non-solid cell found and record the fallback on `Player.spawnWarning`.

PER-AXIS COLLISION (D-06 — locked). Implement `Player.canOccupyX(nx, y)` and `Player.canOccupyY(x, ny)` as private-ish helpers, then `Player.moveBy(dx, dy)`:
  - For the X attempt: let `edge = nx + (dx > 0 ? RADIUS : -RADIUS)` be the leading edge; the move is rejected if `Level.isSolid(floor(edge), floor(y - RADIUS))` OR `Level.isSolid(floor(edge), floor(y + RADIUS))` — both corners of the leading edge, so the radius protects the corners as well as the centre line.
  - The Y attempt is the mirror image using the `y`-leading edge and the two x-extreme corners: `edge = ny + (dy > 0 ? RADIUS : -RADIUS)`, rejected if `Level.isSolid(floor(x - RADIUS), floor(edge))` OR `Level.isSolid(floor(x + RADIUS), floor(edge))`.
  - RESOLUTION ORDER, stated explicitly because it is the difference between sliding and a subtle jitter bug: resolve X FIRST and commit it (write `Player.x` if accepted, leave it alone if rejected), THEN resolve Y. The `x` passed to `canOccupyY` is the POST-X-RESOLUTION value — the x the player actually holds at that moment — never the pre-move x. This is the standard formulation and is what makes the slide land where the player expects.
  - "Independent" means only this: a REJECTED X attempt does not cancel the Y attempt, and vice versa. It does NOT mean Y is evaluated against stale pre-move state — Y is always tested against the current, committed x.
  - A zero delta on an axis skips that axis's test entirely.

UPDATE. `Player.update(dt, intent)` consumes the intent contract `{forward, strafe, turn, run, mouseDX}` (all numeric except `run`, a boolean):
  - Turning: apply `intent.turn * TURN_SPEED * dt` (keyboard turn, delta-time scaled) and then `intent.mouseDX * MOUSE_SENSITIVITY` (mouse turn, event-based, NOT delta-time scaled) through `Player.rotate`. Skip the call entirely when the total is zero.
  - Movement: build the local intent vector from `forward` and `strafe`. Derive the unit RIGHT vector by dividing the plane by `CONFIG.FOV_PLANE` — the plane is the authoritative source of "right", and dividing out the field-of-view length is what makes strafe speed equal forward speed regardless of FOV. Normalize the `(forward, strafe)` pair when both are non-zero so a diagonal is not faster than a straight line. Multiply by `WALK_SPEED`, by `RUN_MULT` when `intent.run` is set, and by `dt`. Feed the resulting world delta into `moveBy`.
  - Guard non-finite `dt` or intent values by returning without moving, so one bad number can never corrupt the pose.

TUNNELING BUDGET. Expose `Player.maxStepPerFrame()` returning `WALK_SPEED * RUN_MULT * CONFIG.DT_MAX`. Add a comment stating the invariant: because the collision test is at the LEADING EDGE of the destination and walls are one cell thick, tunneling requires a single-frame step of at least one full cell, and this value must stay well below 1.0. Do not hardcode the number — derive it, so retuning the speed constants keeps the invariant honest.

  <files>Doom/Claude Opus 4.8/GSD/js/game.js, Doom/Claude Opus 4.8/GSD/js/main.js, Doom/Claude Opus 4.8/GSD/index.html</files>

Create `js/game.js` attaching a global `Game` object — the orchestrator that owns the frame.

STATE. `running` (boolean), `last` (previous frame timestamp in milliseconds), `dt` (last clamped delta in seconds), `time` (accumulated simulated seconds), `frames` (count), `rafId`, and `resync` (a boolean flag described below).

SEAMS (this is why the loop is written before its consumers exist). `Game.input` defaults to `null` and, when assigned by Plan 03, must be an object exposing `readIntent()` returning the intent record. `Game.view` defaults to `null` and, when assigned, must be an object exposing `render()` that writes into `Framebuffer.buf32` and does NOT present. `Game.ZERO_INTENT` is a frozen intent record with `forward`, `strafe`, `turn`, `mouseDX` all 0 and `run` false, used whenever no input source is attached.

FRAME (PLAT-04, D-05 — locked). `Game.frame(now)`:
  - Return immediately when not running.
  - Compute the delta. When `resync` is set, clear the flag and take `dt = 0` for this frame — the clock-domain-safe way to absorb a tab refocus without mixing timestamp sources. Otherwise compute `raw = (now - last) / 1000`; coerce a non-finite or negative value to 0 (a backwards clock must never move the player backwards); then `dt = raw > CONFIG.DT_MAX ? CONFIG.DT_MAX : raw`. THE CLAMP IS THE WHOLE POINT: an unclamped delta after a tab switch or a garbage-collection pause is the documented tunneling source, and the clamp is what bounds one frame's movement to well under one cell.
  - Store `last = now`, accumulate `time += dt`, increment `frames`.
  - A RESYNC FRAME SKIPS `Game.step` ONLY. It still calls `Game.render()` and still presents, exactly like every other frame. Do not early-return past the render — `Game.start()` itself sets `resync`, so an early return would make the very first frame after start paint nothing, which is a visible black flash, and it would break the invariant that a present happens exactly once per frame. Stepping with dt 0 would be harmless arithmetically; skipping the step is the cheaper way to say the same thing, and skipping the RENDER is what would actually be wrong.
  - So: when not resyncing call `Game.step(dt)`; always call `Game.render()`; then schedule the next frame. Frames presented equals frames run, unconditionally.
  - Bind the frame callback once at module scope and reuse it, so scheduling allocates nothing per frame.

STEP. `Game.step(dt)` reads the intent — `Game.input ? Game.input.readIntent() : Game.ZERO_INTENT` — and passes it with `dt` to `Player.update`. Nothing else this phase; later phases add entity and weapon updates here.

RENDER. `Game.render()` calls `Game.view.render()` when a view is attached, otherwise fills the framebuffer with `CONFIG.CLEAR_COLOR`; then performs exactly ONE `Framebuffer.present()`. Keeping the present in the loop rather than in each view is what guarantees Phase 1's single-blit-per-frame contract survives every future view swap.

CONTROL. `Game.start()` sets `running`, sets `resync`, and schedules the first frame; it is idempotent (a second call while running is a no-op). `Game.stop()` clears `running` and cancels the pending frame. `Game.attach()` registers the resync triggers: a `visibilitychange` listener that sets `resync` when the document becomes visible, and a window `focus` listener that does the same. Both must also call `Game.input.reset()` when the attached input source exposes one, so a key held at the moment focus was lost cannot stay stuck down — belt and braces alongside the clamp.

WIRING. In `js/main.js`, on window load: initialise the framebuffer as today, build the textures and sprites as today, then call `Level.build()`, `Player.spawn()`, `Game.attach()` and `Game.start()`. Stop calling `Preview.render()` as the persistent screen — the running loop now owns what is on screen — but leave `js/preview.js` loaded, since `Preview.blit` remains the reference color-keyed blit for Phase 4. The resize handler must call `Framebuffer.resize()` only; the loop repaints on the very next frame, so no manual repaint is needed any more.

Register `js/player.js` and `js/game.js` in `index.html` as classic script tags: `js/player.js` immediately after `js/level.js`, and `js/game.js` after it but still before `js/main.js`. Extend the load-order contract comment to name both new files, state that `Game.input` and `Game.view` are the two seams Plan 03 fills and Phase 3 re-points at the raycaster, and state that the delta-time clamp lives in `CONFIG.DT_MAX`.
  </action>

  <name>Task 3: Motion harness — dt clamp, frame-rate independence, no tunneling under a two-second hitch, wall sliding</name>
  <files>Doom/Claude Opus 4.8/GSD/tools/verify-motion.cjs</files>
  <action>
Create `tools/verify-motion.cjs`: a Node harness built on `tools/boot.cjs` that loads config, framebuffer, textures, sprites, preview, level, player and game into the vm, builds the level, spawns the player, and drives `Game` through the manually-stepped animation-frame scheduler. It attaches a scripted intent source to `Game.input` so the harness controls exactly what the player is trying to do each frame. Print one `PASS`/`FAIL` line per assertion and the terminal token `ALL_MOTION_CONTRACTS_PASS` only when every assertion passed.

Anchoring rule for every assertion below: unless the assertion names a different landmark, re-place the player at `Level.LANDMARKS.openCell` and reset the direction before measuring, so no distance measurement is silently clipped by a wall the harness did not know was there. Never search the grid for a suitable cell and never hardcode map coordinates — Plan 01 derives and verifies these anchors precisely so this harness can name them.

Assertions:
1. Clamp constant — `CONFIG.DT_MAX` is 0.05.
2. Clamp behaviour (PLAT-04) — drive frames whose raw deltas are 16 ms, 2000 ms (a two-second hitch), 100000 ms, 0 ms, a negative delta and a non-finite delta; after every one assert `Game.dt` is finite, at least 0 and at most `CONFIG.DT_MAX`, and that `Player.x`/`Player.y` stayed finite. Additionally assert the resync contract at the loop boundary: the FIRST frame after `Game.start()` is a resync frame, so `Game.dt` is exactly 0 and the pose is unchanged after it, `Game.resync` is clear afterwards, the second frame advances the pose normally, and the present counter still incremented on that first frame — a resync frame skips the step, never the render.
3. Max-step invariant — `Player.maxStepPerFrame()` is strictly less than 0.5, i.e. comfortably under the one-cell step that tunneling through a one-cell-thick wall would require. This is the ARITHMETIC half of the no-tunneling proof; assertions 5 and 6 supply the empirical half.
4. Frame-rate independence (PLAT-04) — from the open-cell anchor, hold forward for one simulated second delivered as 60 frames of 16.67 ms, then reset and repeat as 250 frames of 4 ms; assert the two travelled distances agree within 2% and both are within 2% of `WALK_SPEED`.
5. No tunneling under a two-second hitch (CTRL-04, D-05) — this assertion must be capable of FAILING for the reason it exists, so it tests SIDE PRESERVATION against one named wall face rather than the legality of the resting cell. Note explicitly why the weaker form is worthless: Plan 01 guarantees every non-solid cell is reachable from spawn, so "non-solid, in bounds, in the reachable region" is equally true of a player that successfully tunneled, and would pass. Instead read `L = Level.LANDMARKS.wallFaceEast`, place the player at `x = L.x`, `y = L.y` — half a cell from the face, on the open side, centred in its row — and set the direction to due east `(1, 0)` via `Player.setDir`. Hold forward WITH the run modifier, deliver ONE frame with a raw delta of 2000 ms, then 200 more frames of 16 ms. After EVERY one of those frames assert all three: `Player.x < L.wf - Player.RADIUS + 1e-9` — the leading edge never reached the face, so the player is provably on the near side of THAT specific wall; `floor(Player.x) === L.mx`; and `floor(Player.y) === L.my`. Then repeat the entire sequence with the hitch frame delivered after 30 normal frames of run-speed motion (so the player is already moving when the hitch lands) and with a 100000 ms hitch instead of 2000 ms, asserting the same three conditions each time.
6. Never inside geometry, and never skipping a cell (CTRL-04) — drive 5000 frames of pseudo-random intent generated from `mulberry32(CONFIG.SEED)` (random forward/strafe/turn/run each frame, mixing 16 ms frames with occasional 2000 ms hitch frames) starting from the open-cell anchor. Capture the player's cell BEFORE and AFTER each frame as `(pmx, pmy)` and `(mx, my)`, and after EVERY frame assert:
   (a) both coordinates are finite and `Level.isSolid(mx, my)` is false — never inside geometry;
   (b) no cell was skipped on either axis: `abs(mx - pmx) <= 1` AND `abs(my - pmy) <= 1`. Because `maxStepPerFrame()` is under 0.5 cells, a jump of 2 on an axis is not reachable by legal motion — it can only mean the player teleported through a wall, which is precisely the tunneling event assertion 5 tests deterministically and this one tests across a randomized drive;
   (c) no corner was cut: a frame that changes BOTH cell coordinates is legal — a diagonal move can cross an x boundary and a y boundary in the same frame, so a Manhattan step of 2 is NOT on its own a failure and must not be flagged as one — but the path taken must have been legal. Since X resolves before Y, the intermediate cell the resolver actually passed through is `(mx, pmy)`; assert it is non-solid. That establishes a real 4-connected path `(pmx,pmy) -> (mx,pmy) -> (mx,my)` and rules out a diagonal slip through a solid corner.
7. Wall sliding (CTRL-04, D-06) — read `C = Level.LANDMARKS.corridorCell`, place the player at `C.x`, `C.y`, and set the direction with `Player.setDir` to the normalized sum of the unit vector pointing into one of the two blocked-axis walls and `C.slideDir`, so a forward-only intent drives diagonally into the wall with room to slide. Run 60 frames of 16 ms holding forward. Assert the blocked-axis coordinate moved by less than 1e-6 while the free-axis coordinate moved by more than 0.3 — the rejected axis stayed put while the free axis kept going, which is the slide. Then take `L = Level.LANDMARKS.wallFaceEast`, place the player at `L.x`/`L.y` facing east, and drive straight into the face for 120 frames; assert `Player.x < L.wf - Player.RADIUS + 1e-9` throughout, that `floor(Player.x)` is still `L.mx`, and that the final 20 frames changed the position by less than 1e-9 — the player is at rest against the wall, not oscillating or creeping into it.
8. Diagonal is not faster (CTRL-01) — one frame of forward-only and one frame of forward-plus-strafe from the same pose produce displacement magnitudes equal within 1e-9.
9. Run modifier (CTRL-01) — one second of forward with the run modifier travels `RUN_MULT` times the walk distance, within 1%.
10. Strafe geometry (CTRL-01) — a strafe-right frame displaces the player along the plane direction (dot product of the normalized displacement with the normalized plane above 0.999) and a strafe-left frame displaces it the opposite way; strafe distance equals forward distance for the same duration within 1e-9.
11. Camera invariants (D-01) — after 1000 rotations by pseudo-random angles assert the direction is unit length, the plane length equals `CONFIG.FOV_PLANE`, their dot product is zero, and `planeX` equals `dirY * FOV_PLANE` and `planeY` equals `-dirX * FOV_PLANE` — all within 1e-6.
12. Turn is delta-time scaled, mouse is not — one simulated second of `turn = 1` rotates by `TURN_SPEED` radians within 1e-6 regardless of whether it is delivered in 60 or 500 frames; and a total `mouseDX` of 300 produces the same total rotation whether delivered in one frame or spread over 50 frames.
13. Loop contract — the harness's putImageData counter increments exactly once per frame across every drive in this harness, resync frames and hitch frames included, so the total present count equals the total frame count with no exceptions; and with `Game.input` and `Game.view` both null the loop still runs 30 frames without throwing and still presents once per frame.

Compare floating-point values with explicit tolerances, never with equality.
